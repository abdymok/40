Note on the fatality audit

The fatality dashboard went live on October 10. The preliminary audit uses daily casualty roundups, including Ukraine24h's visual maps, as an external cross-check for missed incidents and possible duplicates. Its independently compiled totals are not used as a target for this project's count.

The comparison has identified reports of deaths that have not yet been matched to specific chronology entries. That does not automatically mean those deaths are missing from the numerical subtotal. Daily roundups may cover rolling 24-hour periods rather than calendar days, report deaths from earlier attacks after victims die in hospital, or repeat figures already included in previous updates.

We are checking each case against its underlying incident and source before changing the tally. The dashboard's published figure remains a provisional documented subtotal for selected incidents, not a complete national count. Unresolved reports will not be silently discarded, but neither will they be added without checking for overlap.

The method page now explains this approach and distinguishes a documented death from an unresolved report or a possible duplicate. The audit continues. Every fatality matters, and the count deserves the same care as the reporting behind it.

Ballistic warning timing

A general Kyiv air alert does not establish that a ballistic threat was identified with useful lead time. On October 18–19, the Air Force said partners gave no warning before the first ballistic strike, information for the second arrived with roughly one minute to react. Air Force account, Oct. 19. Follow official Air Force alerts, the tracker records alert intervals, not a guarantee of advance ballistic warning.

Russia has carried out 5,796 attacks on Ukraine's energy system since 2022 – report

Ukraine · Green Deal Ukraine · November 6, 2026

Green Deal Ukraine says Russia had carried out 5,796 attacks on Ukraine's energy system as of February 2026. Its report, Energy Resilience by Design: Ukraine's Wartime Experience and Learnings for the EU, says available conventional generation capacity fell to 10.5-12 GW at the height of the winter 2025/26 attacks, against estimated peak demand of about 18 GW.

Based on 15 interviews conducted in September 2026 and desk research, the study examines six areas of energy resilience, including infrastructure protection, strategic equipment reserves, rapid repairs, decentralized generation and public communication. It recommends stronger crisis coordination, critical equipment stocks and greater use of island-capable local power systems.

Source: Green Deal Ukraine, Energy Resilience by Design: Ukraine's Wartime Experience and Learnings for the EU, November 2026.

Experts familiar with the power system told Energoreforma that Kyiv had effectively been operating in emergency outage mode for several days because of a shortage of generation and severely constrained transmission from outside the capital after Russian strikes on combined heat-and-power plants and substations. The experts said the scale of Russian attacks made it impossible to guarantee uninterrupted service through preparation alone. Their assessment also raised questions about the deployment of alternative power sources and passive protection at critical facilities. These are attributed expert comments, not a published technical assessment of the remaining capacity in Kyiv's grid.

Sources: Energy Ministry and Ukrenergo statements reported by Reuters, November 6, Ukrenergo official Telegram channel, LB.ua on the suspension of tram and trolleybus services, November 3. Expert comments were reported by Energoreforma in an Interfax-Ukraine dispatch, November 6.

Russian attacks destroy 800,000 sq m of warehouse space in Q3 – NBU

Ukraine · National Bank of Ukraine · November 5, 2026

The National Bank of Ukraine recorded 153 cases of deliberate damage to warehouse facilities as a result of Russian attacks in July-September 2026. Based on open sources, the regulator estimates that about 800,000 square meters of warehouse space were destroyed in the third quarter alone. Cumulative losses since 2022 have reached 2.1 million square meters, according to the NBU's October Inflation Report.

The NBU says the destruction has increased logistics costs for businesses. Market estimates cited in the report show that rents for quality warehouse space rose by 12-13% over several months. The attacks have forced businesses to diversify storage, use longer transport routes and hold smaller batches of inventory, while damage to stocks and supply disruptions have limited the availability of some goods.

The regulator expects the impact on production to weaken as businesses adapt, although attacks on warehouse and logistics infrastructure are expected to continue into 2027.

Source: National Bank of Ukraine, October 2026 Inflation Report, published November 5, 2026.

Nuclear and scientific infrastructure

Sept. 30 — Institute for Nuclear Research, Kyiv. A Russian UAV struck the grounds of the National Academy of Sciences' Institute for Nuclear Research, where the VVR-M research reactor is located. The State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate reported a fire but no damage to the reactor or safety-important systems, and no change in radiation levels, readings remained within the natural-background range of 0.16–0.20 μSv/h. The reactor has been shut down with its core fully unloaded since February 2022.

Sept. 28 — National Academy of Sciences Presidium, Kyiv. A Russian drone struck the building of the NAS Presidium, causing significant damage and killing two people, according to Ukraine's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The MFA subsequently described the Institute for Nuclear Research strike as part of a wider pattern of attacks affecting Ukrainian nuclear and scientific institutions.

Earlier damage — Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology. The MFA notes that Russian forces repeatedly shelled the National Scientific Center "Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology" in 2022, damaging infrastructure of its Neutron Source nuclear facility.

Sources: State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate of Ukraine, Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine, Oct. 1 statement on attacks against nuclear and scientific institutions, MFA statement on the Sept. 28 strike on the National Academy of Sciences.
