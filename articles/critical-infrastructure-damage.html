Oct. 3North (Pivnichnyi) BridgeA Russian drone struck the North (Pivnichnyi) Bridge in Kyiv at about 6:57 a.m., according to Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko and the Kyiv city administration. The road surface and trolleybus overhead contact network were damaged. Traffic from the left bank to the right bank was initially closed, delaying buses 21, 73, 101 and 114 and trolleybuses 29, 30, 31 and 47. Bus service was later restored on adjusted routes, while replacement trolleybus services were organized on both banks. Repair work on the damaged contact network continued. The strike followed repeated attacks on the South (Pivdennyi) Bridge on Oct. 1 and 2. By Oct. 3, the city had revised the alert-time traffic rules: South Bridge traffic from the left bank to the right bank is unrestricted during an alert, while right-to-left traffic is restricted for 15 minutes after the alert begins, then resumes. Podilsko-Voskresenskyi Bridge traffic stops in both directions for 15 minutes when an alert begins or ends, then resumes in full. North, Darnytskyi, Paton and Metro bridges operate without alert-related restrictions.
Oct. 2South Bridge / Kyiv bridge networkAt about 5 a.m., a Russian UAV struck the South (Pivdennyi) Bridge again — the fourth reported hit in two days. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said emergency services were heading to the site. The Kyiv City Military Administration later reported that the South Bridge was fully closed to traffic in both directions. Police reported partial restriction to one lane on Metro Bridge from the left bank toward the right bank, and a complete restriction on Paton Bridge for traffic from the right bank toward the left bank. Later on Oct. 2, a further strike produced a fresh smoke plume over the bridge area. The photograph is firsthand documentation and does not by itself establish the precise impact point. Separately, debris fell in Holosiivsky district, damaging windows in a non-residential building and several cars; no fire or casualties were initially reported there. The renewed strikes and closures followed three reported UAV strikes on the South Bridge on Oct. 1.
Oct. 2Ukrainian energy facilities — several regionsAccording to Ukrenergo, Russian strike drones attacked energy facilities in several regions during the previous day and overnight into Oct. 2. By the morning, consumers were without electricity in several areas; Ukrenergo said the most difficult situation was in Sumy and Chernihiv oblasts, where damage to energy facilities was most significant. Emergency restoration work was under way. The operator did not publicly identify individual generating plants or substations in this update, so no specific facility is named here.
Oct. 1South Bridge / Green Line metroRussian drones struck the South (Pivdennyi) Bridge area repeatedly. Two impacts were reported near the support structure, followed later by a strike on the roadway. Reported damage included a bridge support, crash barrier, roadway and metro fence. Metro and surface transport across the bridge were halted during inspections. Surface public transport later resumed, while metro-related inspections continued. Initial reporting said specialists found no critical structural damage in the preliminary inspection; this did not establish the status after the further strike or the renewed attack on Oct. 2. Reports identifying the drones as Geran-5 were not officially confirmed.
Sept. 30Institute for Nuclear ResearchA Russian UAV struck the grounds of the Institute for Nuclear Research of the National Academy of Sciences in Kyiv at 18:18 on Sept. 30, according to Ukraine's State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate. A fire broke out, but the VVR-M research reactor and safety-important systems were not damaged, and radiation readings remained at natural-background levels. The reactor has been shut down since February 2022 with its core fully unloaded; current use is limited to regulated maintenance of safety systems.
Sept. 30Kyiv energy infrastructureRussia launched a large-scale missile and drone attack on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, with power facilities around Kyiv among the reported targets. The attack caused power outages and disrupted other critical services in the capital. Ukrainian officials said critical infrastructure was hit in several regions. The full extent of damage to individual Kyiv generation facilities was not publicly identified.
Sept. 30Darnytska CHP / energy generationDarnytska combined heat and power plant has sustained significant damage from Russian shelling during the war. The plant supplies heat to more than 1,100 residential buildings in Kyiv's Darnytsky and Dniprovsky districts. This entry is included as a major Kyiv generation facility affected by Russian attacks, with the damage and restoration status requiring continuing updates.

Nuclear and scientific infrastructure

Sept. 30 — Institute for Nuclear Research, Kyiv. A Russian UAV struck the grounds of the National Academy of Sciences' Institute for Nuclear Research, where the VVR-M research reactor is located. The State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate reported a fire but no damage to the reactor or safety-important systems, and no change in radiation levels; readings remained within the natural-background range of 0.16–0.20 μSv/h. The reactor has been shut down with its core fully unloaded since February 2022.

Sept. 28 — National Academy of Sciences Presidium, Kyiv. A Russian drone struck the building of the NAS Presidium, causing significant damage and killing two people, according to Ukraine's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The MFA subsequently described the Institute for Nuclear Research strike as part of a wider pattern of attacks affecting Ukrainian nuclear and scientific institutions.

Earlier damage — Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology. The MFA notes that Russian forces repeatedly shelled the National Scientific Center "Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology" in 2022, damaging infrastructure of its Neutron Source nuclear facility.

Sources: State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate of Ukraine; Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine.
