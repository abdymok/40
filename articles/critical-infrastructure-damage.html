Note on the fatality audit

The fatality dashboard went live on October 10. The preliminary audit uses daily casualty roundups, including Ukraine24h's visual maps, as an external cross-check for missed incidents and possible duplicates. Its independently compiled totals are not used as a target for this project's count.

The comparison has identified reports of deaths that have not yet been matched to specific chronology entries. That does not automatically mean those deaths are missing from the numerical subtotal. Daily roundups may cover rolling 24-hour periods rather than calendar days, report deaths from earlier attacks after victims die in hospital, or repeat figures already included in previous updates.

We are checking each case against its underlying incident and source before changing the tally. The dashboard's published figure remains a provisional documented subtotal for selected incidents, not a complete national count. Unresolved reports will not be silently discarded, but neither will they be added without checking for overlap.

The method page now explains this approach and distinguishes a documented death from an unresolved report or a possible duplicate. The audit continues. Every fatality matters, and the count deserves the same care as the reporting behind it.

Ballistic warning timing

A general Kyiv air alert does not establish that a ballistic threat was identified with useful lead time. On October 18–19, the Air Force said partners gave no warning before the first ballistic strike, information for the second arrived with roughly one minute to react. Air Force account, Oct. 19. Follow official Air Force alerts, the tracker records alert intervals, not a guarantee of advance ballistic warning.

Russian attacks kill four in Dnipropetrovsk region

Post 23:21

Kharkiv reports 36 Russian strikes in a week, six wounded in latest attack

Kharkiv · Official

Mayor Ihor Terekhov said Russian forces struck Kharkiv 36 times during the week, injuring 84 people and killing none. Six city districts came under attack, 27 times by FPV drones. Other weapons included Molniya and Banderol drones, guided bombs and Iskander missiles.

The heaviest damage came overnight on Nov. 3, when a strike hit Hospital No. 18 and around 20 apartment buildings in Nemyshliansky district. The hospital sustained serious damage and its patients were transferred to other facilities. The city has restored the heating enclosures in the damaged buildings and is repairing the premises and equipment.

On Nov. 9, drone attacks hit Kyivsky and Kholodnohirsky districts. A Shahed strike in Kholodnohirsky district started a fire in a private house, injured six people and damaged around 10 cars. In Kyivsky district, a drone hit a car and a second strike followed.

Sources: Ihor Terekhov, Kharkiv mayor, November 9 · Kharkiv City Council, November 9.

Post Nov. 9, morning

Russian attacks leave consumers without power in 12 regions

Ukraine · Official

Ukrenergo reported power outages in Vinnytsia, Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zhytomyr, Zaporizhia, Kyiv, Odesa, Poltava, Rivne, Kharkiv, Khmelnytskyi and Chernihiv regions following Russian attacks on energy facilities. The situation remained most difficult in Dnipropetrovsk and Kyiv regions.

Kyiv changes traffic on three Dnipro bridges

Kyiv, Dnipro crossings · Official

Reserve traffic arrangements took effect on three bridges connecting Kyiv’s left and right banks, following additional security concerns and damage to the North Bridge. On Paton Bridge, three of five lanes carry traffic toward the right bank from 07:00 to 10:00 and toward the left bank from 16:00 to 20:00.

Kremenchuk, Poltava region · Attributed

Air Force warns of hostile interference with Air Alert app

Ukraine · Attributed

Ukraine’s Air Alert application sent false warnings of radiation, nuclear, chemical and street-fighting threats late on Sunday. The first message, declaring a radiation threat in Kyiv, appeared at 22:47. The Ukrainian Air Force said the application had probably been subjected to hostile interference and that there was no radiation threat. Military intelligence also reported a failure in the application and urged users to verify air-raid information through other sources. In Lviv region, the radiation-hazard signal was broadcast over loudspeakers.

The State Emergency Service said the national warning system was operating normally: threat signals were being transmitted to regional administrations and onward to street warning systems. It urged people to follow official local-authority information and loudspeaker signals, avoid third-party links and refrain from spreading unverified claims. The incident affected the app, not the operation of the national warning system.

Sept. 30 — Institute for Nuclear Research, Kyiv. A Russian UAV struck the grounds of the National Academy of Sciences' Institute for Nuclear Research, where the VVR-M research reactor is located. A Russian drone struck the building of the NAS Presidium, causing significant damage and killing two people, according to Ukraine's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The MFA subsequently described the Institute for Nuclear Research strike as part of a wider pattern of attacks affecting Ukrainian nuclear and scientific institutions.

Earlier damage — Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology. The MFA notes that Russian forces repeatedly shelled the National Scientific Center "Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology" in 2022, damaging infrastructure of its Neutron Source nuclear facility.
