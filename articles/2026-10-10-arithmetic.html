The arithmetic of inconvenience

The arithmetic of inconvenience

October 10, 2026 · Kyiv

Trump has announced his diesel deal with Putin. The U.S. midterm elections are on November 3. Let the gentlemen enjoy their arithmetic.

In Kyiv, the lights went out around noon yesterday and returned at 05:30 this morning. About seventeen and a half hours. The water is back, though under protest. Drones have been flying for hours.

The ASUS Zenbook is down to 30%. The iPhone 11 is below half charge. A CHOETECH power bank offers 65 watts, which tells us how quickly it may deliver energy, not how much it has stored. My USB-C connector is at work, where it is enjoying a quieter morning. HERSELF has an iPhone and a MacBook. And Vodafone would like another 125 hryvnias for home mobile internet.

One begins to suspect that modern civilisation is held together by cables whose whereabouts are imperfectly understood.

Our governing equation:

Edelivered < Estored

Energy is lost in conversion. Time is lost looking for connectors. Money is lost buying solutions to problems created by other solutions.

For the Zenbook:

Eremaining = 0.30 CASUS

where CASUS is its full battery capacity in watt-hours. The remaining question is how fast we spend what remains, and what we can recharge before the next interruption.

The inventory is short: iPhone 11, Zenbook, 65W power bank, cables and adapters, rechargeable headlamp, removable bicycle lights, HERSELF’s devices, and the Vodafone question.

We shall do the sums. Outside, other people are doing theirs.

The lights are back. The water is running. The laptop is counting down.

I have been pondering the situation on the balcony, watching a beautiful sunrise over Kyiv.

Perhaps the best use of the remaining 30% is to get dressed and walk downtown to retrieve the errant USB-C connector.

I may update later today. Or I may not.
