Methodology, evidence and corrections

How the Kyiv and selected Ukrainian critical infrastructure chronology is compiled.

This is a working editorial record based on reports available to us. It is not a comprehensive incident database, a live monitoring service or an official national count.

Scope and time

The chronology records confirmed, officially reported, attributed and eyewitness reports of damage to Kyiv and selected Ukrainian critical infrastructure. It includes relevant context from September 30, 2026. Dashboard reporting begins October 1. All times are Kyiv local time unless an entry says otherwise.

The chronology is arranged newest first by date. Entries without a verified time are not necessarily ordered within the day. Older entries and their stable anchors are preserved when the chronology is reorganized or archived.

Sources and attribution

Sources are linked as directly as practical. Reported claims are attributed to the person, agency, operator, company or outlet that made them. An official statement establishes what that institution reported. It does not automatically establish facts beyond the statement. Media reports and other sources are used to add context and to compare or reconcile accounts.

Firsthand Kyiv observations are primary evidence of what the observer personally saw, heard, photographed or experienced. They do not by themselves establish a weapon, cause, impact, geographic scope or casualty figure that was not directly observed.

We distinguish observation from inference, retain uncertainty where evidence is incomplete and do not infer a weapon, impact, exact location, damage or casualty figure from footage or sounds unless the evidence supports it.

Evidence labels

  • Official — a statement or record issued by a public authority or government body.
  • Operator or company — information issued by an infrastructure operator, utility or other company.
  • Media — reporting published by a news organization.
  • Eyewitness — a firsthand observation or account.
  • Attributed — a claim assigned to an identifiable speaker or source but not independently established in the entry.
  • Tracker — information from an alert, monitoring or incident-tracking service.
  • Unverified — a report for which the available evidence is insufficient to treat the claim as established.
  • Editorial — analysis, compilation or a clearly identified editorial note.

A label describes the source or evidentiary status of a report. It is not a guarantee that every underlying claim has been independently verified.

Fatality figure

The dashboard's Deaths documented in tracked incidents figure is a provisional subtotal of reported deaths in selected incidents recorded in this project since October 1. It is not a complete national count, an official national total or a UN-style casualty-verification exercise.

We count reported deaths unless the person is specifically identified as military personnel. Police officers, rescuers and other emergency personnel are included. Injuries and people merely listed as missing are excluded.

We compare available official and reliable media reports and avoid adding overlapping incident totals to broader regional or citywide subtotals. A citywide month-to-date figure is not treated as a new incident count. Where the available reports do not establish whether a person is already included in another total, we disclose the uncertainty and do not add the person separately pending reconciliation.

The figure is a documented minimum, not a complete national total. Coverage is uneven and may be especially incomplete in frontline and occupied areas. We do not add an invented allowance for presumed underreporting. The figure and its cutoff date should be read together. Detailed calculations and source links are in the fatality methodology note.

For a separate, independently compiled daily record of civilian deaths and injuries from Russian attacks, see Ukraine24h. Its daily summaries and multi-day totals are a separate record, not a target for matching this project's running minimum.

Kyiv air alerts

The Kyiv alert count and cumulative time under alert are provisional working figures assembled from recorded Kyiv intervals and checked against alert trackers. They are not Ukraine-wide totals and do not imply continuous live monitoring.

Each distinct Kyiv alert is counted once. Intervals are reconciled against the existing baseline, including alerts that cross midnight. Where available, cumulative time uses the duration reported by alerts.in.ua consistently, even when tracker rounding differs by a minute from subtracting displayed clock endpoints. Unresolved older intervals or intervals with uncertain duration are excluded rather than estimated.

Each published snapshot identifies its cutoff. An ongoing alert may be included as a provisional event, but it must be identified as ongoing and its elapsed duration must not be presented as a completed interval. Calculation details are maintained in the Kyiv alert methodology note.

Sites and bridge figures

The sites measure is a working list of reported facilities or separately identified sites struck, damaged or credibly affected since October 1. An entry is not necessarily one physical structure. Some reports group multiple premises, and some lack street-level identification. Broad-area reports and possible overlaps are not added without better location evidence. See the sites methodology and working list.

The bridge-strike figure counts reported missile hits on Kyiv bridges since October 1, not the number of damaged structures. Reports that do not establish a separate impact are not counted as additional hits. See the bridge methodology note.

Corrections and updates

Figures and descriptions may change as authorities publish revised information, rescue operations continue, or overlapping reports are reconciled. When better evidence changes an entry or a running figure, the chronology and the relevant calculation note are updated. Material changes should be identified in the page's update history.

Unresolved discrepancies are disclosed rather than silently resolved by assumption. We do not add a person or incident to a running total when doing so risks double-counting. Reports that cannot be attributed to an available source, or that lack enough location or incident detail, may be labeled unverified or left out of a working count until better evidence becomes available.
