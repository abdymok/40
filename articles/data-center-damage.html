Kyiv and selected Ukrainian data center and network infrastructure damage

Last updated: 8 October 2026, 15:35 Kyiv time

Confirmed, operator-reported, or attributed reports, September 15–October 8, 2026

Infrastructure · Data centers · Mural · Chronology

Scope: primarily Kyiv, with selected Ukrainian locations where the incident documents the same campaign against data centers, telecom infrastructure or network facilities. The Oct. 4 Ukrtelecom entry is in Dnipro and is included as a selected Ukrainian entry.

AT A GLANCE The chronology currently records more than 20 entries covering named companies, facilities or network systems affected, with operator-reported physical damage accounting for the largest group. Other entries document operational disruption, official or Russian claims, and attacks whose physical effect remains unverified. Several operators have moved services or customer data to backup infrastructure after damage to Kyiv facilities.

CYBERSECURITY CONTEXT Microsoft’s Digital Defense Report 2026 ranks Ukraine third worldwide and first in Europe by the number of organizations and users affected by recorded cyberattacks in the first half of 2026. Ukraine accounted for 4.8% of those affected globally, while Russian state actors accounted for 14% of their recorded activity against Ukraine. Government agencies and services were the largest targeted sector globally, accounting for 27% of observed activity, up from 17% in 2025. Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026.

Oct. 8Yandex data center, Sasovo, Ryazan Oblast, Russiaoperator-reported / network monitoring
In what could be retaliation for Russian strikes on Ukrainian data centers, Ukrainian drones struck a Yandex data center in Sasovo, Ryazan Oblast, causing a fire and forcing the site to suspend operations. Yandex said there were problems with power supply to one Yandex Cloud availability zone. NetBlocks reported a significant disruption to Yandex's network following the attack. The company said its core consumer services remained operational. This entry records the reported physical attack and the independently observed network disruption without characterizing the incident as a broader Russian internet or .ru domain outage.
Sources: Reuters · NetBlocks
Oct. 7Omega Telecomoperator-reported
Omega Telecom said its Kyiv data center is ceasing operations following the Russian attack. The company said specialists are continuing to restore communications channels and Internet access from backup sites. The statement records the shutdown of the Kyiv facility and the transfer of services to alternative infrastructure. It does not provide an independent assessment of the physical extent of damage.
Source: Ukrinform, citing Omega Telecom · Omega Telecom, Facebook
Oct. 4Ukrtelecom, Dniprounverified
Russian forces struck a communications-company premises in central Dnipro on the morning of Oct. 4, causing a fire. The Dnipropetrovsk regional military administration confirmed the attack on a communications company but did not name it. Ukrainian and local reports identified the building as Ukrtelecom, while that identification had not been officially confirmed by the authorities at the time of reporting. Information on casualties and the extent of damage was still being clarified.
Source: UNN
Oct. 1Dline.ua / Dream Lineoperator-reported
Dline.ua said its data center at 28 Predslavynska Street in Kyiv was destroyed in a Russian strike. The company said all services and customer data were transferred to backup sites.
Source: Dline.ua, via Ukrinform
Sept. 30Institute for Nuclear Research / Ukrainian Nuclear Data Centerconfirmed by officials
A Russian UAV struck the grounds of the Institute for Nuclear Research of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine in Kyiv, where the VVR-M research reactor is located. Ukrainian nuclear regulators said the reactor and its safety systems were not damaged and radiation levels remained normal. The institute hosts the Ukrainian Nuclear Data Center (UkrNDC), a subdivision of its Neutron Physics Laboratory, which maintains and compiles nuclear-data resources. This entry records an attack affecting scientific data infrastructure, rather than a conventional commercial data-center strike.
Source: State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate information, via UATOM
Sept. 30De NovoRussian claim
Russia's Defense Ministry said an overnight strike hit the De Novo data center in Kyiv, describing it as a major communications and internet-traffic facility. Reuters reported the claim but said it could not independently verify the extent of damage. De Novo's own website identifies its Kyiv site as a commercial data center and cloud facility. Status: Russian claim, physical damage not independently confirmed.
Source: Reuters
Sept. 29Lanetoperational impact
Lanet temporarily closed its Kyiv customer service center for security reasons, citing the safety of employees and visitors. The provider said its network continues to operate normally and customers can receive assistance remotely through online services and other communication channels. The company has not given a reopening date. This is an operational security measure, not a reported physical strike or confirmed network outage.
Sept. 28Dobrobut / Ukrtelecomconfirmed by officials
Dobrobut confirmed that its branch at 40 Antonovycha Street in Kyiv was hit in the morning attack. Ukrtelecom's Kyiv branch is also officially listed at the same address. This establishes Ukrtelecom's presence at the building, but does not establish that Ukrtelecom equipment or offices were damaged.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 27Varyag videoofficial claim
Russia's 50th Separate Brigade "Varyag" published a video showing strike sequences it identifies as attacks on 11 Ukrainian data-center or network facilities: De Novo, PrivatBank, Kyivstar, United DC, Cosmonova, G50/NEWTELCO, Inter Cars, BeMobile, Datagroup, Parkovy and Volia. Several are independently documented elsewhere in this chronology. The video is evidence of the brigade's claimed strikes, not by itself of the extent of damage or service disruption at each facility.
Source: 50th Separate Brigade Varyag
Sept. 27Kyivstarconfirmed by officials
Kyivstar's press service confirmed that the building housing the company's main office in Kyiv was damaged in the morning attack. The company said no one was injured. This is confirmed damage to telecom infrastructure, not a confirmed separate data-center strike.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 27KyivstarLater, local Telegram channels reported that another Kyivstar data center had been attacked. The report is recorded as preliminary and has not been independently verified.
Sept. 27VodafoneRussian claim
The Russian Defense Ministry reported that a Vodafone data-processing center in Kyiv had been hit, claiming it supports mobile communications and high-speed Internet services for the Armed Forces of Ukraine. Separately, dev.ua reported that the Telegram community MZU said a drone struck a building in Kyiv's Shevchenkivsky district housing a large Vodafone technical office. Vodafone declined to comment to dev.ua. Vodafone's own news page had no statement confirming damage as of the latest check. This remains an attributed claim, not a confirmed physical data-center damage report.
Source: Reuters
Sept. 27Government internet continuityofficial claim
Prime Minister Serhiy Koretsky said Ukraine must plan for the worst-case scenario in protecting internet infrastructure. He said some infrastructure should be in the cloud, some abroad, and some in combinations of the two or all three approaches.
Source: Office of the President of Ukraine
Sept. 27Kyiv data centerofficial statement
President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian forces shelled a data center in Kyiv twice during the morning. The statement does not identify the operator or facility, so the incident is recorded without assigning it to a specific company.
Source: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy
Sept. 26Data centers / critical communications infrastructureofficial statement
Following a meeting of the Staff, President Zelenskyy said additional decisions had been made to protect and ensure the uninterrupted operation of Ukraine's data-center infrastructure and other facilities critical to communications. He said full implementation of the decisions at all levels was the personal responsibility of government officials and the heads of the security services.
Source: Office of the President of Ukraine
Sept. 25Parkovy data centeroperator-reported
Parkovy Data Center said in a statement published on its Facebook page that repeated Russian strikes caused critical damage to its Kyiv site and forced it to cease operations there. The company said services at the site cannot currently be restored, while customer data is held at backup facilities in the EU. This is an operator-confirmed physical data-center damage and shutdown.
Source: Parkovy Data Center statement, via Mezha
Sept. 25Inkom business center / Datagroupunverified
A Russian drone struck the Inkom business center at 31/33 Smolenska Street (now Simyi Brodskykh Street) in Kyiv. The seven-storey, multi-tenant office building houses offices of different companies. Datagroup was reported as a tenant. The building itself is also associated with PrJSC Information Computer Systems (INCOM), whose registered and actual address is 31/33 Smolenska Street. The strike is relevant to the wider sequence of attacks on Kyiv's telecom and data-center infrastructure, but the available reporting does not establish that the entire building was a data center or that every tenant's equipment was hit.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 25National networkofficial statement
Presidential adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov said Ukraine's highly distributed Internet network should prevent a nationwide or citywide loss of Internet access despite attacks on communications infrastructure. He said localized outages remain possible and that repair costs may increase Internet service prices.
Source: Office of the President of Ukraine
Sept. 24Etherlinkoperator-reported
Etherlink reported damage to its main network node. The company said the existing site could not be restored quickly and that it was moving the network core elsewhere.
Source: dev.ua
Sept. 24Domonetoperator-reported
Reported damage to switching equipment following the previous day's attack. Technical specialists said they had found a way to restore the equipment and expected to do so by 21:00.
Source: dev.ua
Sept. 24KIEVNEToperator-reported
Reported damage to one of its data centers, which supports part of its network. The provider said a loss of power could temporarily leave subscribers in central Kyiv without Internet access.
Source: dev.ua
Sept. 24FAUSToperator-reported
Reported infrastructure damage related to the previous day's attack and said it was working to restore its network.
Source: dev.ua
Sept. 24Fex.netoperational impact
Users experienced problems with the cloud service following the attack. The disruption was reported as an operational consequence of damage to Kyiv's digital infrastructure; no separate physical Fex.net facility strike was identified in the available reporting.
Source: Ukrainska Pravda
Sept. 24KyivLinkoperator-reported
Reported damage to one of its technical sites and said it was working to restore normal network operation. Some users could experience temporary disruptions or unstable Internet access.
Source: dev.ua
Sept. 24AMCUconfirmed by officials
The Antimonopoly Committee said damage to infrastructure, including Internet providers, prevented timely publication of public-procurement appeal decisions. It said its services were fully restored by 13:30.
Source: Ukrainska Pravda
Sept. 24NCECconfirmed by officials
The telecom regulator's website remained offline. NCEC said it was working to restore it and warned of possible disruptions to communications networks and services amid continuing attacks on telecommunications infrastructure.
Source: Ukrainska Pravda
Sept. 23–24Cityhostoperator-reported
Cityhost reported damage to equipment in one Kyiv data center. The company began restoring services from backup equipment abroad.
Source: DeHealth review of the reported incident
Sept. 23UTELSoperator-reported
UTELS reported damage to its Kyiv data center, where its principal network equipment was located. Equipment was left without power and service was disrupted.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 23Pavutynaoperator-reported
Pavutyna reported damage to one of its data centers and a major network emergency.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 23Crazy Networkoperator-reported
The company reported a missile strike on its central Kyiv internet-traffic-exchange data center/node. Customers in Kyiv, Vinnytsia and Khmelnytsky regions were affected.
Source: h.ua
Sept. 23MiroHostoperator-reported
Founder Oleksandr Olshanskyi said the Kyiv data center containing MiroHost's Ukrainian capacity was destroyed in the Russian attack. The company could not give a recovery timeline. Its foreign infrastructure continued operating, covering roughly 95% of its customers.
Source: Ukrainska Pravda, citing MiroHost founder
Sept. 23Judicial information systemsoperational impact
A disruption at a backbone provider made Ukrainian court services unavailable from 13:58 to 16:17. Services were partially restored, with stabilization work continuing.
Source: Ukrainska Pravda
Sept. 19De Novoreported
De Novo's Kyiv data center was reportedly struck. It is one of Ukraine's largest commercial data centers. The strike has been reported by data-center industry sources; the available reporting does not give us a detailed operator damage assessment.
Source: Data Center Dynamics
Sept. 17Kyivstarconfirmed by officials
A building containing Kyivstar equipment in Kyiv region was damaged, Kyivstar said in a statement posted on Facebook. The company said customer services continued normally. This was infrastructure damage, not a confirmed data-center hit. Kyivstar had also reported damage to an engineering office on Sept. 11.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine
Sept. 15BeMobile / UCloudoperator-reported
BeMobile data center in Kyiv was affected by a drone attack. UCloud confirmed its Kyiv data center was affected; sites using UCloud, including the National Paralympic Committee site, experienced disruption. The extent of physical damage was not disclosed.
Source: Data Center Dynamics
