Trump’s Newsmax remarks on Ukraine: a defining moment

The significance is not simply that Donald Trump says he dislikes President Z. It is the political definition of the war that the US president is offering in public — and the one he leaves out.

The definition being offered

Trump calls Z “not a good guy for us to deal with,” says he finds him more difficult to deal with than Vladimir Putin, and argues that Ukraine’s attacks on Russian refineries are a “disgrace” because they raise the world oil price. He says there are “plenty of other targets” Ukraine could hit. He also says Ukraine needs an election, invoking missile strikes on apartment buildings as a reason to question whether its people are satisfied with their president.

Taken together, these are not merely personal insults. The president of the United States publicly casts the leader of the invaded country as the harder obstacle to a settlement, treats the aggressor’s hostility toward that leader as a practical problem to be managed, and puts the world’s need for diesel alongside — and ahead of any explicit account of — Ukraine’s need to defend itself.

Trump does not formally announce in this interview that US support for Ukraine has ended. But the signal is unmistakable: blame is directed at Kyiv, the legitimacy of its wartime president is put in question, and Putin is presented as the easier negotiating partner. That is a public distancing from Ukraine, delivered by the person whose country has been its most consequential military backer.

The defining moment is therefore also a contest over definition. Is this a war of Russian aggression against a sovereign country, whose elected leadership is trying to keep it alive? Or is it a dispute in which the invaded country’s president is the problem because he will not accommodate the aggressor — and because Ukrainian resistance inconveniences global oil markets? Trump is offering the second account. The record of the invasion does not support it.

A related Reuters report

In a report published on October 16, Reuters said that an order to limit Starshield, the satellite communications service used by Ukraine’s military, came from inside the White House, citing two US officials and a Western official briefed on the matter. A senior Ukrainian official told Reuters that Kyiv was informed the decision had been made at the White House.

Ukrainian officials said the restrictions complicated strike missions and slowed interceptor drones used against Russian Shahed attacks. A senior US official told Reuters that “no such request was ever made by the president.” The report did not identify who inside the White House initiated the restriction, and it does not establish that Trump personally ordered it.

The report is relevant context for the public pressure described above: Reuters places the reported restriction amid Washington’s demands that Kyiv stop striking Russian refineries. That timing is notable, but the article does not establish that the restriction and Trump’s Newsmax remarks were coordinated.
