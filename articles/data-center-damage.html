Kyiv data center and network infrastructure damage

Confirmed, operator-reported, or attributed reports, September 15–27, 2026

DateCompany / facilityWhat happened
Sept. 27KyivstarKyivstar's press service confirmed that the building housing the company's main office in Kyiv was damaged in the morning attack. The company said no one was injured. This is confirmed damage to telecom infrastructure, not a confirmed separate data-center strike.
Sept. 27KyivstarLater, local Telegram channels reported that another Kyivstar data center had been attacked. The report is recorded as preliminary and has not been independently verified.
Sept. 27VodafoneThe Russian Defense Ministry reported that a Vodafone data-processing center in Kyiv had been hit, claiming it supports mobile communications and high-speed Internet services for the Armed Forces of Ukraine. Separately, dev.ua reported that the Telegram community MZU said a drone struck a building in Kyiv's Shevchenkivskyi district housing a large Vodafone technical office. Vodafone declined to comment to dev.ua. Vodafone's own news page had no statement confirming damage as of the latest check. This remains an attributed claim, not a confirmed physical data-center damage report.
Sept. 27Kyiv data centerPresident Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian forces shelled a data center in Kyiv twice during the morning. The statement does not identify the operator or facility, so the incident is recorded without assigning it to a specific company.
Sept. 26Data centers / critical communications infrastructureFollowing a meeting of the Staff, President Z said additional decisions had been made to protect and ensure the uninterrupted operation of Ukraine's data-center infrastructure and other facilities critical to communications. He said full implementation of the decisions at all levels was the personal responsibility of government officials and the heads of the security services.
Sept. 25Inkom business center / DatagroupA Russian drone struck the Inkom business center at 31/33 Smolenska Street (now Simyi Brodskykh Street) in Kyiv. The seven-storey, multi-tenant office building houses offices of different companies. Datagroup was reported as a tenant. The building itself is also associated with PrJSC Information Computer Systems (INCOM), whose registered and actual address is 31/33 Smolenska Street. The strike is relevant to the wider sequence of attacks on Kyiv's telecom and data-center infrastructure, but the available reporting does not establish that the entire building was a data center or that every tenant's equipment was hit.
Sept. 25National networkPresidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said Ukraine's highly distributed Internet network should prevent a nationwide or citywide loss of Internet access despite attacks on communications infrastructure. He said localized outages remain possible and that repair costs may increase Internet service prices.
Sept. 24EtherlinkEtherlink reported damage to its main network node. The company said the existing site could not be restored quickly and that it was moving the network core elsewhere.
Sept. 24DomonetReported damage to switching equipment following the previous day's attack. Technical specialists said they had found a way to restore the equipment and expected to do so by 21:00.
Sept. 24KIEVNETReported damage to one of its data centers, which supports part of its network. The provider said a loss of power could temporarily leave subscribers in central Kyiv without Internet access.
Sept. 24FAUSTReported infrastructure damage related to the previous day's attack and said it was working to restore its network.
Sept. 24Fex.netUsers experienced problems with the cloud service following the attack. The disruption was reported as an operational consequence of damage to Kyiv's digital infrastructure; no separate physical Fex.net facility strike was identified in the available reporting.
Sept. 24KyivLinkReported damage to one of its technical sites and said it was working to restore normal network operation. Some users could experience temporary disruptions or unstable Internet access.
Sept. 24AMCUThe Antimonopoly Committee said damage to infrastructure, including Internet providers, prevented timely publication of public-procurement appeal decisions. It said its services were fully restored by 13:30.
Sept. 24NCECThe telecom regulator's website remained offline. NCEC said it was working to restore it and warned of possible disruptions to communications networks and services amid continuing attacks on telecommunications infrastructure.
Sept. 23–24CityhostCityhost reported damage to equipment in one Kyiv data center. The company began restoring services from backup equipment abroad.
Sept. 23UTELSUTELS reported damage to its Kyiv data center, where its principal network equipment was located. Equipment was left without power and service was disrupted.
Sept. 23PavutynaPavutyna reported damage to one of its data centers and a major network emergency.
Sept. 23Crazy NetworkThe company reported a missile strike on its central Kyiv internet-traffic-exchange data center/node. Customers in Kyiv, Vinnytsia and Khmelnytsky regions were affected.
Sept. 23MiroHostFounder Oleksandr Olshanskyi said the Kyiv data center containing MiroHost's Ukrainian capacity was destroyed in the Russian attack. The company could not give a recovery timeline. Its foreign infrastructure continued operating, covering roughly 95% of its customers.
Sept. 23Judicial information systemsA disruption at a backbone provider made Ukrainian court services unavailable from 13:58 to 16:17. Services were partially restored, with stabilization work continuing.
Sept. 19De NovoDe Novo's Kyiv data center was reportedly struck. It is one of Ukraine's largest commercial data centers. The strike has been reported by data-center industry sources; the available reporting does not give us a detailed operator damage assessment.
Sept. 17KyivstarA building containing Kyivstar equipment in Kyiv region was damaged. Kyivstar said customer services continued normally. This was infrastructure damage, not a confirmed data-center hit. Kyivstar had also reported damage to an engineering office on Sept. 11.
Sept. 15BeMobile / UCloudBeMobile data center in Kyiv was affected by a drone attack. UCloud confirmed its Kyiv data center was affected; sites using UCloud, including the National Paralympic Committee site, experienced disruption. The extent of physical damage was not disclosed.

Working chronology, newest first. Compiled by abdymok. Company-reported infrastructure damage is not necessarily evidence of a separate physical data-center strike. Downstream service outages are listed separately from physical damage reports. The Sept. 25 Inkom entry is based on reporting that Datagroup occupied space in the multi-tenant Inkom business center, and on public registration data identifying PrJSC Information Computer Systems (INCOM) at the same address. It does not establish that all tenants or all equipment in the building were affected. The Sept. 27 Vodafone entry records an attributed claim and reported damage to a Vodafone technical-office building, but not a confirmed physical data-center strike.
