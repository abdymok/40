Data center and network infrastructure damage in Ukraine and Russia

Last updated: 26 October 2026

Confirmed, operator-reported, or attributed reports, September 15–October 26, 2026

Infrastructure · Kyiv cauldron · Data centers · Mural · Chronology

Scope: primarily Kyiv, with selected Ukrainian and Russian locations where incidents document attacks on data centers, telecom infrastructure or network facilities. Russian entries are included as reported incidents and context for the cross-border campaign, with claims attributed to the relevant companies or sources. The Oct. 4 Ukrtelecom entry is in Dnipro.

AT A GLANCE The chronology currently records more than 20 entries covering named companies, facilities or network systems affected, with operator-reported physical damage accounting for the largest group. Other entries document operational disruption, official or Russian claims, and attacks whose physical effect remains unverified. Several operators have moved services or customer data to backup infrastructure after damage to Kyiv facilities.

CYBERSECURITY CONTEXT Microsoft’s Digital Defense Report 2026 ranks Ukraine third worldwide and first in Europe by the number of organizations and users affected by recorded cyberattacks in the first half of 2026. Ukraine accounted for 4.8% of those affected globally, while Russian state actors accounted for 14% of their recorded activity against Ukraine. Government agencies and services were the largest targeted sector globally, accounting for 27% of observed activity, up from 17% in 2025. Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026.

Oct. 26Ukraine’s banking system / data-center resilienceNBU governor’s statement
National Bank of Ukraine Governor Andriy Pyshnyy said Russian attacks on data centers had not substantially affected the banking system, but had exposed gaps in some banks’ business-continuity testing protocols. He said banks and the NBU work through attack scenarios and must test their procedures routinely. Pyshnyy cautioned that 100% protection against wartime damage cannot be guaranteed and that intensified attacks could cause temporary disruption to parts of the network. He also called for discussion of strategic autonomy and said sovereign cloud infrastructure should be among the first topics. The remarks point to resilience planning and cloud architecture as part of the banking sector’s response to physical attacks on digital infrastructure.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine interview with NBU Governor Andriy Pyshnyy at the IMF and World Bank Group Annual Meetings in Bangkok, October 26, 2026.
Oct. 20 · reported Oct. 21GigaCenter data center, Kyivoperator-reported · damage
GigaCenter said its Kyiv data center was damaged in a Russian attack on October 20. The company warned that customers may experience disruptions to equipment hosted at the facility and said it was working to contain the consequences of the strike. The extent of physical damage and any service interruptions had not been specified in the report. GigaCenter provides colocation for business servers and IT equipment and is part of GIGAGROUP, alongside GigaTrans, GigaCloud and GigaSafe.
Source: GigaCenter statement on Facebook, reported by Interfax-Ukraine, October 21, 2026.
Oct. 19 · 10:18Datagroup data center, Kyivoperator-reported · total destruction
Datagroup's press service said the company's data center in Kyiv had been completely destroyed and could not be restored. The statement was relayed by the Realna Ukraina Telegram channel at 10:18 on October 19. The report establishes the company's stated assessment of the facility; the post supplied for this entry does not identify the exact site or provide further technical details.
Source: Realna Ukraina | Radar Tryvoha, Telegram, Oct. 19, 10:18, citing Datagroup's press service
Oct. 14 · morningStarshield / Ukrainian military satellite communicationsoperational disruption reported by Ukrainian officials and corroborated by Reuters
Ukrainian officials and military sources reported that restrictions on in-motion satellite terminals disrupted drone missions overnight on October 13–14. Presidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said the military-grade Starshield service had been limited at speeds above 160 km/h and that Ukrainian forces had received no advance warning. Reuters cited a source in the 7th Rapid Reaction Corps who confirmed connection drops and mission cancellations. The exact scope, technical implementation and rationale for the change remain unclear. Starlink’s public help page says certain in-motion speed limits in Ukraine are implemented in coordination with the Ukrainian government for national-security and public-safety reasons; that general policy statement does not establish the details or timing of this specific change.
Sources: Reuters, October 14, reporting operational disruption · Serhii Beskrestnov (“Flash”), October 14 · Starlink Help Center, in-motion speed restrictions in Ukraine · Ukrainska Pravda, October 14, frontline-source reporting and video description.
Oct. 14 · 12:38Russian online services / downstream network impactsingle-channel report · scope not independently confirmed
Pravda Gerashchenko reported that Russian banks, marketplaces, food-delivery services and the Gosuslugi government portal were broadly inaccessible, including services on Russia’s so-called “white list.” The report is consistent with the wider pattern of downstream disruption following damage to three Yandex data centres in Sasovo, Kaluga and Vladimir, which has affected services and businesses relying on Yandex infrastructure. Russian financial publication Expert wrote on October 14 about continuing Yandex Pay problems and the risks that service interruptions pose to digital financial infrastructure. The Moscow Times reported partial restoration of Yandex Pay on October 13, while noting continued problems with Yandex Pro and Yandex Cloud. These reports corroborate ongoing disruption in parts of the ecosystem, but do not independently confirm this specific claim of a fresh, broad outage at 12:38, especially the failure of white-listed services. Outage-monitoring signals checked for this update were mixed and did not establish a nationwide failure of all the named categories.
Sources: Pravda Gerashchenko, Telegram, report at 12:38 · Meduza, Oct. 12, on outages following three Yandex data-centre attacks · Le Monde, Oct. 13, on downstream service disruptions · DownRadar, Gosuslugi status and user reports · Expert, October 14, financial-infrastructure analysis · The Moscow Times, October 13, Yandex Pay partial recovery
Oct. 12 · reportedStarlink ground communications site, Sofia area, Bulgariareported allegation · official confirmation pending
Bulgarian MP Ivaylo Mirchev said two masked men set fire to 11 of 17 antennas at a site near Sofia that he described as providing a backbone connection to SpaceX’s Starlink satellites, including for communications used by Ukraine. Bulgarian National Television reported the allegation on October 13. The reported number is not consistent across coverage: another Bulgarian report cited an unnamed source putting the number at nine. As of the latest reports consulted, the relevant authorities had not publicly confirmed the full account, the extent of damage or the perpetrators. Mirchev pointed to a similar reported incident in Poland, but a connection between the cases has not been established here. The allegation is recorded as a potential attack on communications infrastructure, not as a confirmed disruption to Ukrainian service.
Sources: Bulgarian National Television, Oct. 13 · European Pravda, Oct. 14 · Stolitsa.bg, reporting discrepancy in the number of antennas
Oct. 12Yandex data centres / Russian governmentofficial acknowledgment / operator-reported impact
Russian Digital Development Minister Maksut Shadayev told a State Duma committee that the attacks on Yandex data centres were unprecedented in scale. He said his ministry and the Defence Ministry were discussing ways to protect such facilities. Separately, Yandex said it was still assessing damage at the affected sites and could not give a reliable timetable for restoring the damaged infrastructure. Some services remained unavailable or impaired at the time of reporting. The minister’s remarks confirm that the government is discussing protection measures; they do not independently establish who carried out each attack or the full extent of damage.
Sources: Interfax Russia, Oct. 12, reporting Shadayev’s remarks · Yandex statement, Oct. 9 · Yandex Cloud incident status
Oct. 12Yandex data centres / online information environmentpresidential statement / not independently verified
Speaking by video to the Warsaw Security Forum, President Z said that after Ukraine’s response to Russian attacks on Ukrainian data centres, attacks on Yandex data centres coincided with a reduction in hate, division and provocation on social media. He attributed the change to fewer bots and said it had become clearer where they originated. This is Z’s assessment, not a verified measurement of bot activity. The reported service disruption at Yandex does not by itself establish who operated the accounts or prove the cause of any change in online discussion.
Source: Interfax-Ukraine, Oct. 12, reporting Z’s remarks
Oct. 11Yandex / Russian data-centre marketindustry reporting / forecast
Following reported damage or shutdowns at Yandex data centres in Sasovo, Kaluga and Vladimir, Kommersant reported that equipment replacement could take up to six months. Citing unnamed industry sources, it said Yandex was expected to seek equipment through multiple channels, including foreign suppliers, and that short-term pressure on hardware prices could follow. These are market-source expectations, not a confirmed Yandex procurement announcement or evidence that prices have already risen. The report does not establish that foreign purchases would involve sanctions evasion or other “grey” schemes. Separately, Yandex said it had arranged accelerated migration of customer services to VK Cloud, Selectel and K2Cloud, a continuity measure that does not itself replace damaged physical capacity.
Sources: Kommersant, Oct. 11 · Kommersant, Oct. 9, on migration to partner providers · Yandex statement, Oct. 9
Oct. 11Yandex data center, Vladimir, Russiaoperator-reported / attributed attack
Yandex Cloud said a drone attack damaged infrastructure at its data center in Vladimir and that operations at the site had been completely suspended. The company reported no injuries and advised customers to activate alternative disaster-recovery plans while it assessed the damage. The affected availability zone, ru-central1-a, was reported unavailable. Users and outage monitors reported disruptions affecting Yandex services, including Yandex Music and the Alice assistant / smart-home service. This is the third reported Yandex data-center incident in four days, following Sasovo in Ryazan Oblast and Kaluga. The incident and service impact are operator-reported; attribution of the attack is not independently established here.
Sources: Reuters, Oct. 11 · Kyiv Independent, Oct. 11 · Telegram report
Oct. 9Yandex data center, Kaluga region, Russiaoperator-reported / Fire Point claim
Yandex said a drone attack damaged part of its data center infrastructure in the Kaluga region, with several modules put out of operation. Ukrainian drone producer Fire Point said Ukraine’s military used its FP-1 one-way attack drones to strike what it described as Yandex’s largest data center, and said several modules were completely disabled. The company was assessing the damage. Yandex said no employees were injured at either the Kaluga or Sasovo sites. The available reporting supports damage to the facility and partial loss of infrastructure, but the weapon attribution and Fire Point’s description of the site are attributed claims.
Sources: Reuters · Yandex statement, Oct. 9
Oct. 9Government policy / data centres and telecom operatorsofficial announcement
Prime Minister Serhiy Koretsky said the Cabinet of Ministers had simplified access to property owned by business companies in which the state holds more than 50% of the shares. Data-centre and telecommunications operators will be able to lease qualifying property for digital and telecom infrastructure without an electronic auction. The simplified mechanism is to apply throughout martial law and for six months after it ends. Koretsky said the measure is intended to help businesses relocate and deploy IT infrastructure more quickly amid Russian attacks. This records a policy change, not evidence that a specific operator has leased a site or brought new capacity online.
Sources: TrueUA, reporting Koretsky’s announcement
Oct. 8Yandex data center, Sasovo, Ryazan Oblast, Russiaoperator-reported / attributed attack
Yandex said a drone attack overnight on Oct. 7–8 seriously damaged its Sasovo data center, caused a fire and forced the site to stop operating. The company said it could not yet confirm whether the equipment could be restored. Two of Yandex’s three AI supercomputers are housed at the facility, but Yandex has not confirmed whether they were damaged. Ukraine’s military did not publicly comment on this specific strike. President Z said Ukraine was responding “in mirror-like fashion” to Russian attacks on Ukrainian data centers, without naming the facility. The incident disrupted some Yandex services and its cloud infrastructure. Reports also described problems at other online services that rely on infrastructure providers, but the available evidence does not establish a nationwide Russian internet outage.
Sources: Reuters · Yandex statement, Oct. 9 · Data Center Dynamics
Oct. 7Omega Telecomoperator-reported
Omega Telecom said its Kyiv data center is ceasing operations following the Russian attack. The company said specialists are continuing to restore communications channels and Internet access from backup sites. The statement records the shutdown of the Kyiv facility and the transfer of services to alternative infrastructure. It does not provide an independent assessment of the physical extent of damage.
Source: Ukrinform, citing Omega Telecom · Omega Telecom, Facebook

Information on casualties and the extent of damage was still being clarified.
Source: UNN

Sept. 30Institute for Nuclear Research / Ukrainian Nuclear Data Centerconfirmed by officials
A Russian UAV struck the grounds of the Institute for Nuclear Research of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine in Kyiv, where the VVR-M research reactor is located. Ukrainian nuclear regulators said the reactor and its safety systems were not damaged and radiation levels remained normal. The institute hosts the Ukrainian Nuclear Data Center (UkrNDC), a subdivision of its Neutron Physics Laboratory, which maintains and compiles nuclear-data resources. This entry records an attack affecting scientific data infrastructure, rather than a conventional commercial data-center strike.
Source: State Nuclear Regulatory Inspectorate information, via UATOM

Status: Russian claim, physical damage not independently confirmed.

The provider said its network continues to operate normally and customers can receive assistance remotely through online services and other communication channels. The company has not given a reopening date. This is an operational security measure, not a reported physical strike or confirmed network outage.

The strike is relevant to the wider sequence of attacks on Kyiv's telecom and data-center infrastructure, but the available reporting does not establish that the entire building was a data center or that every tenant's equipment was hit.
