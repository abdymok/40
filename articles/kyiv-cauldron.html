14 October 2026 · Verkhovna Rada · Mayor’s account

Klitschko takes Kyiv’s winter preparations — and the fight over the city — to parliament

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko addressed the Verkhovna Rada after lawmakers summoned him to report on the city’s preparation for winter. His appearance came on the morning of a mass Russian ballistic attack on energy facilities in Kyiv and the region. At 07:15, Klitschko reported electricity interruptions in the capital and said the ballistic threat continued. The separate infrastructure chronology records the attack and its reported consequences.

Klitschko said the city had continued preparing under attack. He put the number of missiles and drones directed at Kyiv’s CHP-5 and CHP-6 at almost 50 so far in 2026, compared with 19 in 2025, 12 in 2022, seven in 2023 and none in 2024. These are the mayor’s figures for attacks on the two plants; this page does not independently reconcile the annual counts.

His inventory of municipal backup provision included:

  • Nearly 700 generators of different capacities prepared for heat- and water-supply facilities, and 70 mobile boiler units.
  • Three built diesel power stations and a reserve water intake.
  • 103 generators at Kyivvodokanal, with larger generator systems still being manufactured abroad for the city.
  • More than 6,000 generators, inverters and batteries purchased for residential buildings through municipal energy-support programmes.
  • Generators at all educational institutions, including 285 high-capacity units.
  • 480 generators at municipal hospitals; 15 hospitals with their own wells and six more building them; 11 medical facilities with autonomous heating from their own boiler houses, with 21 expected to have it by year-end.

The inventory describes equipment and projects reported by the mayor. It does not establish that every unit is installed, fueled, connected, available at the required capacity or sufficient to replace grid supply during a prolonged outage. Backup equipment for individual facilities is not the same as a substitute for the city’s interconnected electricity, heating and water systems.

Klitschko also made a political charge. He said that central-government payment freezes had delayed energy-security projects in Kyiv and other communities, and rejected accusations that the capital was disrupting the heating season, which had not yet begun. He asked where the air defence for the capital was, where protection for Ukrenergo facilities was, and where the billions allocated for that protection had gone. Those are his allegations and questions, not findings established by this chronology.

His broader argument was that Kyiv was being asked to keep functioning through repeated attacks while central authorities publicly questioned its preparations and, in his account, obstructed work through frozen payments. As head of the Association of Ukrainian Cities, he extended the complaint beyond the capital, saying other municipalities were facing the consequences too.
